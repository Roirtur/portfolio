Research internship

adtool

TopicsCuriosity-driven exploration / IMGEP / Interference identification

Inria Bordeaux / FLOWERS team

My internship at Inria Bordeaux was part of AIxIA, a project studying timing interference in multicore embedded systems. Two programs can work fine on their own, then slow each other down when they share a cache or memory controller. In a real-time system, that delay matters.

I brought Ludovic Matar's curiosity-driven prototype into ADTool, the FLOWERS team's open-source library for exploring complex systems. We generate pairs of small memory-access programs, run each one alone and then together in a dual-core simulator, and describe the differences through timing, cache and DDR measurements. IMGEP uses those observations to search for different behaviours.

The internship grew beyond that first experiment. I turned it into a reusable way to explore systems whose inputs are programs, and reworked parts of ADTool that made experiments hard to resume, compare or inspect.

I also helped a researcher at IRCAM set up ADTool to explore a space generated by a synthesiser. Seeing it used for something so different made the value of that rework much more concrete to me.

What I did

  • I separated program generation, goal sampling, mixing, mutation, simulation and behaviour encoding into components that can be changed through configuration.
  • I compared the integrated experiment with the reference using coverage plots. On the tested simulator and settings, IMGEP showed the same broad behaviour and covered more of the selected regions than random exploration.
  • I built a general program-based layer, so another project can bring its own program format, simulator and operators while reusing ADTool's exploration loop.
  • I rebuilt experiment history around checkpoints, so a run can be saved, resumed or branched from an earlier point.
  • I made a new interface to follow a run, inspect and export discoveries, and compare results. I also added coverage views and DDR-access heatmaps.
  • I separated the library from its examples, repaired packaging and imports, and added documentation and smoke tests.
